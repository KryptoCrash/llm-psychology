\begin{abstract}
LLMs increasingly operate in multi-agent and conversational settings, and recent
behavioral work shows that they can conform to a unanimous incorrect majority,
mirroring Asch's classical findings in humans. We study this behavior in
Qwen3-8B on questions the model answers correctly without social context, then
ask whether the resulting conformity can be seen and manipulated inside the
model\footnote{Released repository: \url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/llm-psychology-D32E/README.md}}. Repeated incorrect peer answers pull the model away from otherwise
available answers, with conformity increasing as the apparent consensus grows;
simple prompt changes such as explanation prompting and question distillation
reduce the effect. In activation space, we find a late-middle layer residual-stream
direction associated with incorrect consensus pressure. Steering along this
direction can both increase and reduce conformity, suggesting that
peer-majority conformity is supported by a detectable and steerable internal
feature.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large language models are routinely placed in collaborative settings: they
summarize previous answers, review other agents' outputs, and participate in
multi-agent workflows. A model may know an answer in isolation, but abandon reasoning due to pressure to conform. Prior work adapts
classic social-psychology paradigms to LLMs and finds that this can happen
\citep{asch1951effects,zhu2025conformity}, with sensitivity to group size,
unanimity, and question difficulty
\citep{bellina2026conformity,zhong2025disentangling}. If conformity matters for deployed multi-agent systems, we also want
to know whether the social-pressure signal is visible and manipulable inside the
model.

We make three claims about Qwen3-8B. First, on questions the model normally answers correctly, repeated incorrect peer answers reliably cause conformity, and simple prompt changes partly mitigate the effect.
Second, a residual direction at the assistant-start
token tracks this pressure in activation space, especially at late-middle model layers. Third, steering along this direction
causally changes behavior. We find limited evidence for a steerable feature in one model and prompt family.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\paragraph{LLM conformity and social compliance.}
Asch's classical experiments showed that a unanimous incorrect majority can
drive human conformity \citep{asch1956studies}. \citet{zhu2025conformity}
translate this paradigm to LLMs, finding that many models conform to unanimous
incorrect majorities and that paraphrasing and non-unanimity prompts reduce conformity. \citet{weng2025benchform} formalize related
multi-round protocols in BENCHFORM. Conformity is adjacent to sycophancy:
\citet{perez2023sycophancy} and \citet{sharma2023sycophancy} study agreement
with users, and recent mechanistic work finds separable sycophancy directions
\citep{vennemeyer2025sycophancy} and mid-to-late-layer overrides of model
knowledge \citep{li2025truthoverridden}. These papers motivate our behavior
metric but do not identify a linear representation of conformity.

\paragraph{Linear features and interventions.}
The linear representation hypothesis predicts that high-level concepts can
appear as recoverable activation directions \citep{park2024linear}. Related
directions have been studied for truthfulness, honesty, and other traits
\citep{burns2023discovering,li2023inference,marks2024geometry}. Because probes
can detect information without identifying the computation that controls a
behavior \citep{alain2017understanding,belinkov2022probing}, we use
interventions as the stronger test. We compute simple DiffMean directions, as in
activation addition and contrastive activation addition
\citep{turner2024activation,panickssery2024caa}, and compare to prior steering
work on refusal and persona traits
\citep{arditi2024refusal,chen2025persona}. Activation oracles
\citep{karvonen2025activation} provide an additional localization signal, while
activation patching and steering test whether a candidate direction causally affects conforming
behavior \citep{meng2022locating,wang2023interpretability}.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}

Ultimately, we find that conformity can be represented internally in a model, and that this representation generalizes to a variety of prompt structures. We are limited in that this representation is only partially causal when applied across datasets. However, we believe that with larger diversity in data, residual stream directions computed using DiffMean can fully represent data-agnostic conformity in models.

Our results show a clear distinction between representations of conformity and sycophancy in models, with activation oracles specifically describing conforming beliefs in models when applied to computed representations. Further, projection analysis shows that these representations correlate with properties of conformity, like the size of a peer majority and the propensity for a model to reason independently. Finally, we show that activation patching steers models to specifically feel pressure towards conforming to others. We hope that future work in this direction enables us to learn more about how models learn and act in various social settings; we see this line of research as a means to understand model ethics and social reasoning within multi-agent systems.
